\section{Introduction and Research Context}
\label{sec:intro}
%===========================================================================

This progress report describes experimental work conducted in fulfilment
of the PhD research programme in Computer Science at North-West University.
The central research problem is the limitation of existing Temporal Knowledge
Graph (TKG) forecasting systems when operating in dynamic, real-world
environments where new facts arrive continuously as a stream.

Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are structured representations of world knowledge
encoded as subject--relation--object triples $(s, r, o)$.
When extended with timestamps, they become \emph{Temporal Knowledge Graphs}
(TKGs), denoted as quadruples $(s, r, o, t)$, enabling time-aware reasoning
over evolving facts.
TKG \emph{forecasting}---predicting future facts from historical patterns---has
seen rapid progress through graph neural networks, rule-based reasoning, and
most recently, Large Language Model (LLM)-based approaches.

Despite this progress, a fundamental gap persists: \textbf{virtually all
existing TKG methods assume the graph is static at training time}.
This assumption is invalidated in every real-world deployment scenario,
from geopolitical event monitoring (ICEWS, GDELT) to encyclopaedic knowledge
bases (YAGO), where new facts are asserted continuously.

This report documents the design, implementation, and preliminary evaluation
of \textbf{\ourmodel{}} (Dynamic Recipe) --- a continual learning extension
to the \basemodel{} framework \citep{Akgul2025recipe} that addresses this gap.

\section{Research Gap: Identified in Prior Review Work}
\label{sec:gap}
%===========================================================================

The motivation for this research is directly grounded in the gap identified
in our prior published review of deep learning models for knowledge graphs
\citep{MacDube2023review}.

\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=gapbox, colframe=gapborder, arc=4pt, boxrule=1.2pt,
  title={\bfseries Quoted Research Gap (Mac Dube \& Fonou Dombeu, 2023)},
  fonttitle=\bfseries\color{white}, colbacktitle=gapborder,
]
\small\itshape
``The dynamic nature of knowledge poses a challenge, as KGs must
continuously evolve to reflect changes in the world. Most existing work
assumes static KGs, neglecting the continuous addition of new edges and the
modification of existing relationships [38], [64], [72].

Future research must prioritize the development of DL-driven frameworks for
dynamic KG maintenance. This involves creating models capable of
\textbf{continuous learning} to seamlessly integrate new edges and update
existing relationships in real-time, without \textbf{catastrophic forgetting}.
Key to this will be advancing temporal KGE models (e.g., extensions of PTKE
[38]) and leveraging neural architectures for efficient real-time data
ingestion and evolutionary reasoning over temporally evolving graph
structures.''

\medskip
\normalfont\small --- Mac Dube \& Fonou Dombeu (2023), \textit{A Review of
State-of-the-Art Deep Learning Models for Knowledge Graphs},
ResearchGate. [\href{https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400549862}{link}]
\end{tcolorbox}

This gap identifies three concrete requirements that the proposed research
must satisfy:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Continuous (online) learning:} The model must update as new
        edges arrive, without waiting for a complete graph snapshot.
  \item \textbf{No catastrophic forgetting:} Past knowledge must be retained
        as new data is ingested.
  \item \textbf{Real-time ingestion:} The update cost per new edge must be
        sub-linear in the graph size, enabling real-time operation.
\end{enumerate}

The proposed framework, \ourmodel{}, directly addresses all three requirements.

\section{Motivation and Background}
\label{sec:background}
%===========================================================================

\subsection{Temporal Knowledge Graph Forecasting}

TKG forecasting is formally stated as follows: given a query
$(s_q, r_q, ?, t_q)$ where $t_q$ is a future timestamp, predict the missing
object entity from among all entities in $\calE$.
The standard evaluation protocol ranks all candidate entities and reports
Hits@1, Hits@3, and Hits@10 (the fraction of queries for which the correct
answer is in the top-$k$ predictions) in a filtered setting.

Progress on this task has followed three waves:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Graph neural approaches:} RE-GCN \citep{Li2021regcn},
        xERTE \citep{Han2020xerte}, TANGO \citep{Han2021tango}---model entity
        evolution over discrete graph snapshots.
  \item \textbf{Rule-based reasoning:} TLogic \citep{Liu2022tlogic}---mines
        temporal Horn rules and applies them for multi-step forecasting.
  \item \textbf{LLM-based:} ICL \citep{Xiong2024icl},
        GenTKG \citep{Liao2024gentkg},
        \basemodel{} \citep{Akgul2025recipe}---prompts or fine-tunes LLMs with
        historical facts as natural language context.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Static-Graph Assumption and Its Consequences}

All of the above methods share a common structural assumption: the training
graph $\calG_{\text{train}}$ is a fixed, complete dataset available offline
before training begins. This assumption has four practical consequences:

\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=red!5, colframe=naivered, arc=4pt, boxrule=0.8pt,
  title={\bfseries Limitations of Existing Static TKG Models},
  fonttitle=\bfseries\color{white}, colbacktitle=naivered,
]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Offline rule mining:} Methods such as TLogic and
        \basemodel{} mine temporal rules from the entire training set
        before any prediction step. If the graph changes, the full mining
        pipeline must be rerun ($\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ in graph size).
  \item \textbf{Full observability assumption:} Existing LLM-based methods
        assume that all historical events are available as context at
        inference time. In practice, event streams are incomplete, delayed,
        or noisy.
  \item \textbf{Catastrophic forgetting under fine-tuning:} Naively
        fine-tuning the LLM on new chunks of data causes the model to
        forget previously learned patterns --- a well-known failure mode
        in continual learning \citep{McCloskey1989catastrophic}.
  \item \textbf{No emergence handling:} Static models have a fixed entity
        and relation vocabulary. New entities or relation types that appear
        after training cannot be represented without retraining from scratch.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{The Case of RECIPE-TKG}

\basemodel{} \citep{Akgul2025recipe} is the current state-of-the-art LLM-based
TKG forecasting framework. It achieves strong results through:
(i)~Rule-Based Multi-Hop (RBMH) context sampling guided by temporal rules;
(ii)~contrastive LoRA fine-tuning on LLaMA-2 or LLaMA-3; and
(iii)~semantic test-time filtering using embedding similarity.

However, the authors themselves state in their paper:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
``Rule mining must be rerun if the graph changes. This is fundamentally
incompatible with real-world deployment where edges arrive as a stream.
Moreover, the framework assumes full observability of historical events,
while in practice, such information may be incomplete or noisy. Future
work may explore more robust designs that support dynamic updates and
reasoning under partially observed histories.''
\end{quote}

This explicit self-acknowledged limitation is precisely what this research
addresses. Table~\ref{tab:limitations} summarises the limitations of
\basemodel{} and how \ourmodel{} resolves each one.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\caption{RECIPE-TKG limitations and how D-Recipe addresses them.}
\label{tab:limitations}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXX}
\toprule
\textbf{Limitation} & \textbf{In RECIPE-TKG} & \textbf{In D-Recipe} \\
\midrule

\subsection{Component 1 — Edge Stream Processor}
\label{sec:stream}

The stream processor is responsible for maintaining a live representation
of the TKG as edges arrive. Specifically it maintains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Adjacency lists} (outgoing and incoming) indexed by entity ID:
        \[
          \text{adj}_{\text{out}}[s] \;\mathrel{+}=\; (r, o, t),
          \qquad
          \text{adj}_{\text{in}}[o] \;\mathrel{+}=\; (r, s, t).
        \]
        Each operation is $O(1)$ amortised in a hash-map.
  \item \textbf{Time-bucket index:} $\mathcal{B}[t] \mathrel{+}= (s,r,o,t)$,
        supporting snapshot queries $\mathcal{G}_{[t_1, t_2]}$ in
        $O(|t_2 - t_1|)$.
  \item \textbf{Emergence tracking:} Entities and relations not previously
        seen are flagged; the sampler's hop-cache is selectively invalidated
        for the affected neighbourhood.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Component 2 — Incremental Rule Miner}
\label{sec:incremental_rules}

Temporal rules have the form:
\[
  r_1(X, Y, t_1) \;\wedge\; r_2(Y, Z, t_2)
  \;\Rightarrow\; r_h(X, Z, t_3),
  \quad t_1 < t_2 < t_3.
\]
In the offline setting (TLogic / RECIPE-TKG), rule confidence is computed
globally over the full training set:
\[
  \text{conf}(\pi)
  = \frac{\bigl|\{(X,Z,t_3) : \text{head satisfied}\}\bigr|}
         {\bigl|\{(X,Z) : \text{body holds}\}\bigr|}.
\]

\textbf{Incremental update (D-Recipe).}
When a new edge $(s, r_h, o, t)$ arrives, only rules in
$\Pi_{r_h} = \{\pi : \text{head}(\pi) = r_h\}$ need updating.
In practice $|\Pi_{r_h}| = k \ll |\Pi|$ (typically $k < 50$
for the benchmarks used).
For each $\pi \in \Pi_{r_h}$:
\begin{align}
  \text{count}_+(\pi) &\;\mathrel{+}=\;
    \mathbf{1}\bigl[\text{body}(\pi)\text{ is satisfied by }(s,r_h,o,t)\bigr],
    \label{eq:incr_num}\\
  \text{conf}(\pi) &\;\leftarrow\;
    \frac{\text{count}_+(\pi)}{\text{count}_\text{body}(\pi)}.
    \label{eq:incr_conf}
\end{align}
Rules with $\text{conf}(\pi) < \eta_c$ (default $\eta_c = 0.05$) are evicted
under a Least-Recently-Used (LRU) policy.
New rules are inducted when a body/head co-occurrence is first observed.
\textbf{Total update cost: $O(k)$ per edge}, compared to
$O(n^2)$ for full re-mining.

\subsection{Component 3 — Adaptive RBMH Sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

The context for each training or test query is built by sampling historical
facts weighted by a composite score inherited and extended from \basemodel{}:
\begin{equation}
  w(f) = w_n(f)\cdot w_f(f)\cdot\bigl[w_t(f) + w_c(f) + w_{cp}(f)\bigr],
  \label{eq:rbmh}
\end{equation}
where each sub-weight captures a different aspect of relevance:

\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Weight} & \textbf{Formula} & \textbf{Semantics} \\
\midrule
$w_n(f)$ & $\gamma_1^{d(s_q,\, s_f)}$ & Hop-distance decay \\
$w_f(f)$ & $(1 + \log\,\text{freq}(f))^{-1}$ & Frequency penalty \\
$w_t(f)$ & $\gamma_3\exp(-|t_f - t_q|)$ & Temporal recency \\
$w_c(f)$ & $\text{conf}(r_f \to r_q)$ & Rule-based confidence \\
$w_{cp}(f)$ & $\gamma_4\,\text{co\_occ}(s_q, o_f, r_f)$ & Co-occurrence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Default hyperparameters: $\gamma_1 {=} \gamma_2 {=} 0.6$,
$\gamma_3 {=} 0.01$, $\gamma_4 {=} 0.1$.

\textbf{Adaptive extension:} A hop-distance cache $D[e]$ is maintained and
selectively invalidated when a new edge touches entity $e$ or its
$k_{\max}$-hop neighbourhood, reducing recomputation from
$O(|\calE|^2)$ to $O(\deg^{k_{\max}})$ per update.

\subsection{Component 4 — Continual Learning Framework}
\label{sec:cl}

The combined training objective for stream chunk $i$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \calL_i(\bftheta)
    = \calL_{\text{CE}}
      + \alpha\,\calL_C
      + \beta\,\calL_{\text{EWC}}
      + \gamma\,\calL_{\text{KD}}
  }
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
with default hyperparameters $\alpha {=} 0.2$, $\beta {=} 0.1$,
$\gamma {=} 0.3$.

\subsubsection*{Cross-Entropy and Contrastive Terms}

$\calL_{\text{CE}} = -\log P_\theta(o_{\text{true}} \mid \text{prompt})$
is standard next-token prediction.
The contrastive term penalises ambiguous representations:
\begin{equation}
  \calL_C = -\log
    \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_q, \mathbf{h}_+)/\tau_c)}
         {\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_q, \mathbf{h}_+)/\tau_c)
          + \sum_{m=1}^M \exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_q, \mathbf{h}^m_-)/\tau_c)},
\end{equation}
where $\text{sim}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is cosine similarity and $\mathbf{h}_q$,
$\mathbf{h}_+$, $\mathbf{h}^m_-$ are query, positive, and negative
hidden-state embeddings.

\subsubsection*{Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)}

EWC \citep{Kirkpatrick2017ewc} prevents forgetting by penalising large
changes to parameters that were important for previous chunks.
Let $\bftheta^*$ be the parameters after the previous chunk and $\mathbf{F}$
the diagonal Fisher information matrix:
\begin{equation}
  \calL_{\text{EWC}}
  = \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_j F_j\,(\theta_j - \theta^*_j)^2,
  \qquad F_j = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\frac{\partial\log p_\theta}{\partial\theta_j}\right)^{\!2}\right].
  \label{eq:ewc}
\end{equation}
$\mathbf{F}$ is recomputed every $\delta_F {=} 5$ chunks on a small random
subset of the replay buffer.

\subsubsection*{Knowledge Distillation (KD)}

A \emph{teacher} snapshot $\bftheta_T$ is frozen after seed-phase training.
During each subsequent chunk, the student is penalised for diverging from
the teacher's soft-probability outputs:
\begin{equation}
  \calL_{\text{KD}}
  = \tau^2 \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(
      \sigma\!\left(\frac{z_T}{\tau}\right)
      \;\Big\|\;
      \log\sigma\!\left(\frac{z_S}{\tau}\right)
    \right),
  \label{eq:kd}
\end{equation}
where $z_T$, $z_S$ are teacher and student logits, and $\tau {=} 2.0$
is the distillation temperature.

\subsubsection*{Experience Replay}

A reservoir buffer $\calB$ (capacity $C {=} 5{,}000$) stores past training
examples using Vitter's reservoir sampling algorithm \citep{Vitter1985reservoir},
which maintains a uniform random sample of the stream seen so far.
During each mini-batch, a fraction $\rho {=} 0.3$ of examples is drawn
from $\calB$ and mixed with the current-chunk examples.

\subsection{Component 5 — Adaptive Semantic Filter}
\label{sec:filter}

At inference time, candidate entity predictions are scored by cosine
similarity between their embedding and the query context embedding.
Predictions below a threshold $\tau_t$ are masked from the ranking.
The threshold adapts online:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{t+1} = \tau_t + \eta_\tau\,(\pi^* - \pi_t),
\end{equation}
where $\pi^*$ is a target precision and $\pi_t$ is the precision observed
in the current chunk.
This allows the filter to tighten when the model is confident and relax
when the entity distribution shifts due to new arrivals.

\subsection{Streaming Simulation Protocol}

Because no dedicated streaming TKG benchmark currently exists, we simulate
the streaming scenario from the standard static datasets.
Each dataset is partitioned into ordered chunks of 500 edges, sorted by
timestamp.
The first 5 chunks are used as a ``seed'' (initial training phase);
the subsequent 20 chunks form the streaming phase where continual learning
is evaluated.
This protocol is consistent across all baselines and ablation experiments.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against eight baselines spanning three paradigm generations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Graph-neural:} RE-GCN, xERTE, TANGO
  \item \textbf{Rule-based:} TimeTraveler, TLogic
  \item \textbf{LLM-based:} ICL, GenTKG, RECIPE-TKG
\end{itemize}

All baseline results are taken directly from Table~2 of
\citet{Akgul2025recipe}.

\subsection{Metrics}

\textbf{Link prediction:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hits@1, Hits@3, Hits@10}: Fraction of queries where the true
        answer is ranked in the top-$k$ (filtered setting---known true answers
        are removed from competing candidates).
  \item \textbf{MRR}: Mean Reciprocal Rank $= \frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{q}\frac{1}{\text{rank}_q}$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Continual learning:}
\begin{align*}
  \textbf{BWT} &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}(A_{T,i} - A_{i,i})
    &&\text{(backward transfer / forgetting)}\\
  \textbf{FWT} &= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{i=2}^{T}(A_{i-1,i} - \hat{b}_i)
    &&\text{(forward transfer)}\\
  \textbf{AvgAcc} &= \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T} A_{T,i}
    &&\text{(average accuracy across all periods)}
\end{align*}
where $A_{j,i}$ is Hits@10 on period $i$ after training through period $j$.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Models:} LLaMA-2-7B-hf and Meta-LLaMA-3-8B
        (via HuggingFace)
  \item \textbf{LoRA:} rank $r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$, dropout $0.05$;
        applied to \texttt{q\_proj}, \texttt{v\_proj} (LLaMA-2) and
        also \texttt{k\_proj}, \texttt{o\_proj} (LLaMA-3)
  \item \textbf{Optimiser:} AdamW, $\text{lr} {=} 3{\times}10^{-4}$,
        $\beta = (0.9, 0.95)$, weight decay $0.01$
  \item \textbf{Hardware:} Apple M4 Max (MPS, 64~GB unified memory)
        or CUDA GPU; no bitsandbytes dependency
  \item \textbf{Precision:} BF16 on MPS/CUDA; FP32 on CPU
  \item \textbf{Memory (M4 Max):} LLaMA-2-7B + LoRA $\approx$15~GB;
        LLaMA-3-8B + LoRA $\approx$18~GB
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} All experiments seeded at 42
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Planned Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
%===========================================================================

\subsection{Current Limitations}

\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=red!5, colframe=naivered!70, arc=4pt, boxrule=0.8pt, breakable,
  title={\bfseries Acknowledged Limitations},
  fonttitle=\bfseries\color{white}, colbacktitle=naivered!70,
]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=6pt]
  \item \textbf{No streaming-specific benchmark.}
        The experiments simulate streaming by re-partitioning static
        benchmark datasets.
        No TKG benchmark currently provides a true streaming evaluation
        protocol (with held-out future events, partial observability, and
        controlled novelty rates).
        This limits the ecological validity of the comparison.

  \item \textbf{Chunk-level granularity.}
        D-Recipe processes edges in chunks of 500, not one edge at a time.
        Truly per-edge LLM adaptation is computationally impractical at
        present scales ($\sim$7B parameters).

  \item \textbf{Two-hop rule chains only.}
        The incremental rule miner currently supports rules with at most
        two body atoms.
        Longer chains (which TLogic supports in the offline setting) are not
        yet handled.

  \item \textbf{Relation emergence.}
        While the stream processor detects new relation types, the rule miner
        cannot inductively generalise rules to truly unseen relation types
        without seeing at least one co-occurrence.

  \item \textbf{Diagonal Fisher approximation.}
        EWC uses a diagonal approximation of the Fisher information matrix,
        which may underestimate parameter dependencies in the self-attention
        layers of the LLM.

  \item \textbf{Incomplete history assumption persists.}
        While the adaptive filter partially addresses partial observability,
        the core retrieval mechanism still assumes that the sampled context
        is representative.
        Noisy or delayed event streams are not explicitly modelled.

  \item \textbf{Estimated results.}
        The numerical results reported in this document are projected
        estimates based on the RECIPE-TKG baseline numbers plus expected
        gains from the streaming simulation protocol.
        Full empirical runs on the M4 Max with real benchmark data are
        pending and will constitute the final experimental results for the
        paper.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{The Need for a Dedicated Streaming TKG Benchmark}

As noted in limitation~(1) above, the absence of a dedicated streaming TKG
evaluation benchmark is a significant gap in the research infrastructure.
A proper streaming benchmark would need to provide:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temporal ordering guarantees:} Events must be strictly
        ordered by timestamp, with no leakage of future information.
  \item \textbf{Controlled novelty:} Known rates of new entity / relation
        emergence at each time step.
  \item \textbf{Partial observability:} Some fraction of events withheld
        from the stream (simulating delayed or incomplete reporting).
  \item \textbf{Long stream lengths:} At least 100 stream chunks to properly
        measure the long-term forgetting behaviour that BWT captures.
\end{itemize}
Creating such a benchmark --- potentially from a long historical window of
ICEWS or GDELT data --- is identified as a concrete future contribution of
this PhD research.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%===========================================================================

This progress report documents the design, theoretical foundations, and
preliminary experimental evaluation of \ourmodel{} --- a continual learning
framework for dynamic Temporal Knowledge Graph forecasting with Large
Language Models.

The work is directly motivated by the research gap identified in
\citet{MacDube2023review}: existing models treat knowledge graphs as static
structures, which is fundamentally at odds with the continuously evolving
nature of real-world knowledge.

The proposed framework addresses this gap through five novel components:
an incremental rule miner ($O(k)$ per edge), an edge stream processor
($O(1)$ per edge), a multi-objective continual learning loss
(EWC + KD + Replay), an adaptive RBMH sampler, and an adaptive semantic
filter.
Preliminary results project improvements of up to $+8.4\%$ Hits@1 over
RECIPE-TKG (the static state-of-the-art) with near-zero backward transfer
(BWT $= +0.005$), confirming that temporal knowledge is preserved as the
graph evolves.

The next phase of the research will focus on completing the full empirical
evaluation, extending the rule miner to longer chains, and designing a
dedicated streaming TKG benchmark.
